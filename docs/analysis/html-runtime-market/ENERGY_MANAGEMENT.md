# Energy infrastructure implications and qualification

KEX/IL-LLM's governed identity and traversal model can support energy management at several levels: local contextual telemetry, consumption forecasting, dispatch preparation, building optimisation, distributed asset coordination and auditable control decisions. Preserving source truth means Australian/other regional observations keep their timestamps, measurement basis, weather/temperature context, uncertainty and evidence when represented globally. Wider visibility does not grant actuator authority or erase local jurisdiction.

A useful architecture is one field per admitted municipality/region/asset scope, with typed relations for authorised cross-field observations or handoffs. Solar, grid and storage fields can carry equal numeric positions without sharing authority. The implemented field-substitution test confirms this within the registered-context continuation resolver. Replication and rehydration preserve the object's identity, while placement can change.

Potential environmental effects include reduced avoidable server work, improved demand scheduling, less unnecessary battery cycling, better use of renewable availability, and deferred hardware replacement. Each effect has its own measurement: rack watts, endpoint joules, marginal regional grid intensity, curtailed energy, battery degradation, control performance and actual displaced procurement. Their effects cannot simply be added when one intervention already accounts for another's benefit.

For AI-assisted energy decisions, deterministic traversal can select relevant source-local state rather than serialising an entire estate. Actual token/GPU savings require a matched inference test with the same model, task success, tokenizer, inference settings and source evidence. No model-token or GPU-FLOP measurement was made in the current enquiry/compiler pilot. Typed boundaries and local evidence reduce specific invalid-state pathways; they do not prove that an external language model cannot hallucinate.

Qualification progression:

1. Observe: ingest bounded, attributed sensor records; retain unit, time, calibration and jurisdiction. Reproduce regional contextual findings.
2. Recommend: generate auditable suggestions; keep missing, stale, contradictory and out-of-domain inputs typed and non-actuating.
3. Shadow control: compare recommendations to actual accepted actions and plant responses without changing equipment.
4. Restricted actuation: independently enforce device/field authority, command bounds, timing, interlocks, emergency/manual override and safe response to sensor/communications/controller failures.
5. Review expansion: independently test authorised cross-field relations and fault domains before extending scope.

HTML packaging is not, by itself, an actuator permission boundary. Qualified KEX field admission, authenticated command routes, host/browser isolation and physical plant protections must act together. Current software checks support origin/context isolation and lineage-valid recovery; physical energy infrastructure safety or regulatory assurance has not been tested by this pilot.
